Writing a plasmid is one thing; finding one somebody already wrote is another. Right now there is
nowhere to look. Every capability a cell could have has to be written by whoever needs it, and
nothing anyone writes goes anywhere afterwards. I want a registry — a place plasmids and genomes
come from, and a place they go to.

Genomes belong there as much as plasmids do. A genome is a set of plasmids attached together, so
it is its own thing to share rather than a synonym for the plasmids in it. Someone who has worked
out the combination a kind of work needs has made something worth passing on, not a list of parts
and instructions for reassembling it.

Two populations, and I want both. Curated plasmids and genomes, there because we put them there
and stand behind them — what someone new reaches for on the first day. And ones users register
themselves, so what is available is not limited to what we thought of.

Two things to do with each. Use what is there, and register your own. A registry that only hands
out a curated set makes people consumers of our imagination. One that only takes submissions is
publishing into a void. Both halves, for both populations, or it is not the thing I am asking for.

Where this is hosted is not what I am deciding here. It could go several ways and I have opinions,
but those are how and not why. What I want settled is that the registry exists, that plasmids and
genomes both live in it, and that people can take from it and put into it.
