Plasmosome development should have a great AI Native SDLC with strict guardrails and well defined
processes for its own development with AI self-improvement loops and graph engineering.

The other intents say what Plasmosome does for the people who run it. This one says how it gets
built.

By graph engineering I mean the loop engineering already practised here, made explicit. The rules
between steps get decided in one conversation and then forgotten; I want them stated as
constraints. A person approving is one of those constraints, not the only one.

Every loop needs a check that can come back negative and whose answer does not come from the agents
being checked — a test that really ran, a measurement, a rule the loop cannot rewrite, or a person's
judgement about what better means. Agents on one model agree cheaply and in volume, so a loop wired
to their agreement does not improve. It converges.

The boundary takes both conditions at once: work here has a check outside the loop, and answers a
question somebody asked. A guard nobody wanted, with a test proving the guard works, has the first
and not the second. So this does not cover an agent noticing something and writing a rule about it,
where the noticing, the rule and the verdict all come from one place. It does not cover how the
kernel behaves; the other intents have that. And mapping shipped work here does not make it work I
asked for.

Start with one loop that has a real verifier, state I can look at, and a hard stop. I am not asking
for a large graph of agents.
